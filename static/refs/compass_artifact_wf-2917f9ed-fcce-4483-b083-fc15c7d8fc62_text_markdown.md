# What FDPM actually is, in 30 citations

**The FDPM apparatus is a partial-commutative-monoid / separation-algebra workflow over a finite-product state vector, dressed in borrowed vocabulary from formal methods it does not satisfy.** Stripping the misnomers, the document's mathematical content reduces to three things every sound CS reference has supported since the 1960s–2000s: (1) Bernstein's 1966 conditions over per-phase read/write footprints; (2) the frame rule of separation logic (Reynolds 2002; O'Hearn 2007) restricted to a static partition; (3) an assumption-revision discipline closer to AGM belief revision than to Lamport-style invariance. The misnomers are real and consequential: π_N is not a projection, step 22 is not a fixed point, "step gates" are not refinement obligations, and single-writer ownership is not parallelism. The document's recent rename from `formal-specification` to `structured-decomposition` is exactly the right move — Parnas and Clements (1986) call this "faking it" honestly — and should be defended, not apologized for. What follows is the citation-by-citation case for each of those statements, organized as the task requested.

---

## 1. Bernstein's conditions and what single-writer actually buys

The original is **Bernstein, A. J. (1966), "Analysis of Programs for Parallel Processing," IEEE Trans. Electronic Computers EC-15(5):757–763, DOI 10.1109/PGEC.1966.264565**. Bernstein states three set-disjointness conditions over input set I(S) (reads) and output set O(S) (writes): W∩W = ∅ (output independence / WAW), W∩R = ∅ (no flow / RAW), R∩W = ∅ (no anti / WAR). The canonical modern treatments are **Hennessy & Patterson, *Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach* (6e, Morgan Kaufmann 2017, ISBN 978-0-12-811905-1)**, Ch. 3 and Appendix C; **Allen & Kennedy, *Optimizing Compilers for Modern Architectures* (Morgan Kaufmann 2001, ISBN 978-1-55860-286-1)**, Ch. 2; **Padua & Wolfe, "Advanced Compiler Optimizations for Supercomputers," CACM 29(12):1184–1201, DOI 10.1145/7902.7904**; and **Feautrier (2011), "Bernstein's Conditions," in *Encyclopedia of Parallel Computing* (Springer), DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-09766-4_521**, which formally generalizes Bernstein from statement texts to operation instances.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS the formal statement; CHALLENGES the implication.** Every authoritative source is unambiguous that single-writer ownership discharges only the WAW clause. RAW and WAR are *defined by reads* and cannot be decided from write-sets alone — Allen & Kennedy §2.2, Feautrier (2011a), Padua & Wolfe §2 are explicit. The FDPM admits this; the literature confirms the admission is load-bearing, not pedantic. **Action**: cite Bernstein (1966) by DOI at the ⊕ definition; add an inline statement that single-writer ownership over partition {Δ_N} enforces only W∩W = ∅; require per-phase read-set declarations as a precondition for any parallelism claim; cite Feautrier (2011) for the modern instance-wise generalization. Notational collision worth flagging: Δ in compiler literature denotes a *dependence-distance vector*, not an increment.

## 2. Separation logic and the frame rule are the right formal home

Primary references: **Reynolds, "Separation Logic: A Logic for Shared Mutable Data Structures," LICS 2002, DOI 10.1109/LICS.2002.1029817**; **O'Hearn, Reynolds, Yang, "Local Reasoning about Programs that Alter Data Structures," CSL 2001, LNCS 2142, DOI 10.1007/3-540-44802-0_1**; **O'Hearn, "Resources, Concurrency and Local Reasoning," TCS 375(1–3):271–307, DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2006.12.035**; **Brookes, "A Semantics for Concurrent Separation Logic," TCS 375(1–3):227–270, DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2006.12.034**; **Bornat, Calcagno, O'Hearn, Parkinson, "Permission Accounting in Separation Logic," POPL 2005, DOI 10.1145/1040305.1040327**.

The FDPM's "single writer per phase, leaves all other components unchanged" is **literally the frame rule** {P} C {Q} ⊢ {P∗R} C {Q∗R} restricted to a finite-product state space. The disjoint-concurrency rule (O'Hearn 2007 §3; Brookes 2007 §6) goes further: if footprints are ∗-separable, then C₁ ∥ C₂ is provably race-free, *closing all three Bernstein clauses simultaneously* — this is precisely the read-set gap from §1. **Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTS, REFINES.** Action: cite Reynolds 2002 and O'Hearn 2007 explicitly; strengthen ownership from "single-writer" to "single-owner of read-write footprint" or, for shared reads, adopt fractional permissions per Bornat et al. 2005. Terminological hazards: "frame," "resource," "local," and "composition" all collide between separation-logic technical senses and product-development English; add a glossary.

## 3. Owicki-Gries and rely-guarantee — what the alternatives demand

**Owicki & Gries, "An Axiomatic Proof Technique for Parallel Programs I," Acta Informatica 6(4):319–340, DOI 10.1007/BF00268134** (and CACM 19(5):279–285, DOI 10.1145/360051.360224); **Jones, "Tentative Steps Toward a Development Method for Interfering Programs," ACM TOPLAS 5(4):596–619, DOI 10.1145/69575.69577**; **de Roever et al., *Concurrency Verification* (Cambridge Tracts 54, CUP 2001, ISBN 978-0-521-80608-4)**; **Vafeiadis & Parkinson, "A Marriage of Rely/Guarantee and Separation Logic," CONCUR 2007, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-74407-8_18**.

Single-writer ownership trivializes O–G non-interference proofs only for write-write cases; reads still need rely conditions. **O–G is non-compositional** (de Roever §8.5) — adding a phase forces re-verification of the whole product, O(N²) interference proofs for an N-phase model. Rely-guarantee fixes this compositionally: each phase Δ_N gets a rely R_N ("environment leaves my read-set stable") and guarantee G_N ("modifies ⊆ component_N"); the parallel rule requires G_i ⊆ R_j. This pair *is* the FDPM's intuition stated correctly. **Action**: cite Owicki–Gries at the single-writer claim with the warning that it discharges only WAW; add an explicit rely/guarantee specification per phase; cite Vafeiadis & Parkinson 2007 for the unified RG+CSL treatment that handles both disjoint-state and shared-read phases.

## 4. "Projection" is mathematically wrong; the correct word is "lens put" or "phase transition"

**Foster, Greenwald, Moore, Pierce, Schmitt, "Combinators for Bidirectional Tree Transformations," ACM TOPLAS 29(3):17, DOI 10.1145/1232420.1232424**; **Bohannon, Pierce, Vaughan, "Relational Lenses," PODS 2006, DOI 10.1145/1142351.1142399**; **Hofmann, Pierce, Wagner, "Edit Lenses," POPL 2012, DOI 10.1145/2103656.2103715**.

**Verdict: CHALLENGES.** A projection in category theory, linear algebra, and functional analysis is by definition idempotent: p∘p = p. The FDPM admits π_N is not. What it actually describes is the *put* side of an asymmetric lens, taking both a state and a delta and producing a new state — and even there the analogy is strained because the FDPM has no paired *get*. The lens laws (GetPut, PutGet, PutPut) are not even formulable for π_N as written. **Action**: rename π_N to τ_N (phase transition) or σ_N (phase update operator) throughout; reserve π for genuine product projections or idempotent extractors. If lens vocabulary is desired, define a paired view_N and prove PutGet; otherwise drop lens citations and adopt plain transition-system semantics.

## 5. Refinement calculi — checklists are not proof obligations

**Wirth, "Program Development by Stepwise Refinement," CACM 14(4):221–227, DOI 10.1145/362575.362577**; **Morgan, "The Specification Statement," ACM TOPLAS 10(3):403–419, DOI 10.1145/44501.44503**, and *Programming from Specifications*, 2e, Prentice Hall 1994, ISBN 0-13-123274-6; **Back & von Wright, *Refinement Calculus: A Systematic Introduction*, Springer 1998, ISBN 978-0-387-98417-9, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4612-1674-2**.

**Verdict: CHALLENGES — categorically.** Refinement is a *semantic* relation S ⊑ S′ between predicate transformers, discharged by proving wp(S, Q) ⇒ wp(S′, Q). FDPM "step gates" are *exit-criteria checklists* — finite enumerations of artifacts that human reviewers tick. A checklist item ("interface signed off") is not a predicate over program states and cannot be discharged by wp-calculation. Worse, FDPM's 22 phases advance *temporally* and *add* state components; refinement chains operate *vertically*, all entries describing the same end-to-end program at increasing detail. **Action**: stop calling the apparatus a refinement calculus. Reframe as **stage-gate engineering** (Cooper, "Stage-gate systems," *Business Horizons* 33(3):44–54, 1990) or genuinely formalize each phase as Morgan's `w:[pre, post]` and discharge the obligations — the latter being substantial new work. Rename "step gate" to "phase gate" or "stage gate."

## 6. Event-B — vertical machine refinement, not horizontal phase sequencing

**Abrial, *The B-Book*, CUP 1996, ISBN 978-0-521-49619-3, DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511624162**; **Abrial, *Modeling in Event-B*, CUP 2010, ISBN 978-0-521-89556-9, DOI 10.1017/CBO9781139195881**; **Abrial et al., "Rodin: an open toolset for modelling and reasoning in Event-B," STTT 12(6):447–466, DOI 10.1007/s10009-010-0145-y**.

Event-B refinement is *vertical*: a concrete machine M′ refines an abstract M via a gluing invariant J(v, v′), with Rodin generating machine-checked proof obligations — INV (invariant preservation), GRD (guard strengthening), SIM (simulation), VAR (variant decreasing for new events), WD (well-definedness), DLF (deadlock freedom). None has any analogue in the FDPM's review checklists. The 22-step FDPM is not Event-B refinement; the analogy is structurally backwards (temporal vs. abstraction-level). **Verdict: CHALLENGES.** Action: cite Event-B only as an explicit *contrast* point. A genuine Event-B encoding would require a single machine modeling the project's evolving artifact-set, one event per phase with guards encoding entry conditions, gluing invariants, a variant for termination, and Rodin discharge — substantial work, not relabeling.

## 7. ⊕ is a partial commutative monoid; this is exactly a separation algebra

**Calcagno, O'Hearn, Yang, "Local Action and Abstract Separation Logic," LICS 2007, DOI 10.1109/LICS.2007.30**; **Mac Lane, *Categories for the Working Mathematician*, 2e, Springer 1998, ISBN 978-0-387-98403-2, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4757-4721-8**; **Moggi, "Notions of Computation and Monads," Inf. & Comp. 93(1):55–92, DOI 10.1016/0890-5401(91)90052-4**; **Plotkin & Power, "Notions of Computation Determine Monads," FoSSaCS 2002, DOI 10.1007/3-540-45931-6_24**; **Swierstra, "Data Types à la Carte," J. Functional Programming 18(4):423–436, DOI 10.1017/S0956796808006758**.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS structurally, REFINES terminologically.** (Δ, ⊕, Δ_∅) is a *partial commutative monoid* (PCM) — closed-when-defined, associative-when-defined under Kleene equality, commutative-when-defined, with total identity. This is the precise structure Calcagno–O'Hearn–Yang (2007, Def. 2.1) name a **separation algebra**, the semantic substrate of separation logic. The Bernstein condition on composability is the disjointness predicate of the separating conjunction. **The FDPM is independently re-deriving 1999–2007 separation-logic foundations.** Action: rename "monoid" to "partial commutative monoid (separation algebra)" and cite CO'HY 2007. A subtle conceptual point: ⊕ is parallel/commutative, but `apply : S × Δ → S` is sequential — the right idiom is *two* operators tied by an exchange law, not a single monad. See §20.

## 8. Petri nets and process calculi — the dependency DAG should be a workflow net

**Petri, *Kommunikation mit Automaten*, PhD thesis, Bonn 1962**; **Murata, "Petri Nets: Properties, Analysis and Applications," Proc. IEEE 77(4):541–580, DOI 10.1109/5.24143**; **Milner, *Communication and Concurrency*, Prentice Hall 1989, ISBN 0-13-115007-3**; **Hoare, *Communicating Sequential Processes*, Prentice Hall 1985, ISBN 0-13-153289-8**; **Newman, "On Theories with a Combinatorial Definition of Equivalence," Annals of Math. 43(2):223–243, DOI 10.2307/1968867**; **van der Aalst, "Verification of Workflow Nets," LNCS 1248:407–426, DOI 10.1007/3-540-63139-9_48**; **van der Aalst et al., "Soundness of Workflow Nets," Formal Aspects of Computing 23(3):333–363, DOI 10.1007/s00165-010-0161-4**.

The FDPM dependency DAG (1→2→…→22) is a *prose* artifact with no formal operational semantics; encoding it as a workflow net (van der Aalst 1997) yields decidable soundness, reachability, and liveness analysis (Murata 1989). The parallelism claim — "Bernstein-disjoint phases commute" — is a **local-confluence claim**; combined with explicit termination it invokes Newman's lemma to give global confluence (Church-Rosser). **Action**: encode the DAG as a workflow net, state soundness explicitly (every started workflow can complete; no dead transitions), and re-cast the parallelism claim as a confluence theorem. CCS suffices for the static case; do not invoke π-calculus unless name mobility is genuinely needed.

## 9. CALM and CRDTs — the FDPM's "monotonicity" is not what they mean

**Hellerstein, "The Declarative Imperative," SIGMOD Record 39(1):5–19, DOI 10.1145/1860702.1860704**; **Ameloot, Neven, Van den Bussche, "Relational Transducers for Declarative Networking," JACM 60(2):15, DOI 10.1145/2450142.2450151**; **Hellerstein & Alvaro, "Keeping CALM," CACM 63(9):72–81, DOI 10.1145/3369736**; **Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero, Zawirski, "Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types," SSS 2011, LNCS 6976:386–400, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-24550-3_29**.

**Verdict: CHALLENGES.** CALM monotonicity is *lattice-theoretic*: state grows in a partial order, full stop. CRDT semilattice updates satisfy s ⊑ u(s) unconditionally; merge is the lattice join. **The FDPM's "amendment supersedes" mechanism is precisely the non-monotone coordination point CALM identifies as requiring synchronization.** Citing CALM as support would inadvertently undermine the document. Action: rename "invariant_monotonicity" to "invariant persistence under explicit revision," or — if a CRDT analogy is desired — embed each invariant together with its amendment history into a versioned-register lattice (the standard MV-Register construction; Shapiro et al. 2011 §3.3) so the *map of histories* grows monotonically even when content changes.

## 10. Dataflow analysis is the right framing for read-/write-sets

**Kildall, "A Unified Approach to Global Program Optimization," POPL 1973, DOI 10.1145/512927.512945**; **Kam & Ullman, "Monotone Data Flow Analysis Frameworks," Acta Informatica 7(3):305–317, DOI 10.1007/BF00290339**; **Aho, Lam, Sethi, Ullman, *Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools*, 2e, Pearson 2006, ISBN 0-321-48681-1**, Ch. 9; **Cousot & Cousot, "Abstract Interpretation," POPL 1977, DOI 10.1145/512950.512973**.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS with caveats.** FDPM's per-phase reads/writes are textbook USE/DEF sets at coarse granularity; the dependency DAG is the corresponding CFG. By Kam–Ullman, monotone-framework iteration terminates at MFP on any finite-height lattice and is sound. The catch: amendment-with-supersession is monotone but **not distributive**, putting the FDPM in the regime where MFP < MOP — tractable but path-imprecise. Action: cite Kildall (1973) and Kam–Ullman (1977); standardize vocabulary (USE/DEF/GEN/KILL); state explicitly that FDPM is a monotone-but-non-distributive framework, sacrificing path sensitivity in a known and accepted way. Optionally cite Cousot–Cousot to formalize abstraction levels via Galois connections.

## 11. ADRs — Nygard is gray literature; cite ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 alongside

**ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022, *Software, systems and enterprise — Architecture description***, IEEE Std 42010-2022; **Kazman, Klein, Clements, *ATAM: Method for Architecture Evaluation*, CMU/SEI-2000-TR-004, DOI 10.21236/ADA382629**; **Kazman et al., "SAAM," ICSE 1994, DOI 10.1109/ICSE.1994.296768**; **Kruchten, Capilla, Dueñas, "The Decision View's Role in Software Architecture Practice," IEEE Software 26(2):36–42, DOI 10.1109/MS.2009.52**; **Tyree & Akerman, "Architecture Decisions: Demystifying Architecture," IEEE Software 22(2):19–27, DOI 10.1109/MS.2005.27**; **Kopp, Armbruster, Zimmermann, "Markdown Architectural Decision Records," ZEUS 2018, CEUR-WS Vol. 2072 (peer-reviewed)**; **Nygard, "Documenting Architecture Decisions," cognitect.com blog, 2011 — gray literature, never peer-reviewed**.

The Nygard template's adoption (Thoughtworks "Adopt", AWS Prescriptive Guidance, GDS Way) reflects pragmatic value, not formal foundation. Standards-track grounding lives in ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010, which in its 2022 second edition introduces formal hooks for architecture decision capture and correspondences for view consistency. **Formal ADR composition / conflict detection across a decision corpus does not exist** — it is an open research area. **Action**: cite ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 as the standards anchor; treat Nygard explicitly as gray literature; consider MADR for structural extension (peer-reviewed at ZEUS); add ATAM/SAAM as the evaluation half; acknowledge formal composition is open research, not delivered by ADR discipline.

## 12. VDM/Z/B/TLA+/Alloy do not have a 22-step decomposition apparatus

**Jones, *Systematic Software Development Using VDM*, 2e, Prentice Hall 1990, ISBN 0-13-880733-7**; **Spivey, *The Z Notation: A Reference Manual*, 2e, 1992, ISBN 0-13-978529-9** (now ISO/IEC 13568:2002); **Abrial, *The B-Book* (1996); *Modeling in Event-B* (2010)**; **Lamport, *Specifying Systems*, Addison-Wesley 2002, ISBN 0-321-14306-X**; **Jackson, *Software Abstractions*, MIT Press, 2e 2012, ISBN 978-0-262-01715-2**.

These are **specification languages and verification frameworks, not project-process models**. Their "stepwise" axis is vertical refinement (data reification, simulation, gluing invariants), not temporal phase sequencing. **Action**: drop any implied formal-methods lineage for the 22-step decomposition. The honest framing is: "FDPM is a process model; for verification of artifacts produced *within* a phase, we recommend B/Event-B (data/safety), TLA+ (concurrency/temporal), or Alloy (structural exploration)." Position formal methods as **tools usable inside phases**, not as ancestors of the process.

## 13. The genuine intellectual lineage is process formalisms

**Osterweil, "Software Processes are Software Too," ICSE 1987, ACM DL 10.5555/41765.41766** (Most Influential Paper); **Wise, Cass, Lerner, McCall, Osterweil, Sutton, "Using Little-JIL to Coordinate Agents in Software Engineering," ASE 2000**; **OMG, *Software & Systems Process Engineering Meta-Model (SPEM) 2.0*, OMG formal/2008-04-01**; **ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017, *Systems and software engineering — Software life cycle processes***.

Little-JIL has rigorously defined operational semantics: pre/post-requisites (analogue of FDPM gates), sequential/parallel/choice/try substep badges (analogue of the FDPM serial chain and parallel tracks), exception handlers with scoped propagation, agent assignment per step, and machine-checkable property verification (FLAVERS). SPEM 2.0 is the OMG metamodel. **Verdict: this is the genuine lineage** — Osterweil, Little-JIL, SPEM. **Action**: re-encode the 22-step model in Little-JIL for verifiability, or in SPEM for tool-supported documentation; cite ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017 as the umbrella life-cycle standard; acknowledge the FDPM's current absence of explicit exception/compensation semantics relative to Little-JIL.

## 14. The critical-path claim is computable but conditional

**Kelley, "Critical-Path Planning and Scheduling: Mathematical Basis," Operations Research 9(3):296–320, DOI 10.1287/opre.9.3.296**; **Kelley & Walker, IRE-AIEE-ACM Eastern '59, DOI 10.1145/1460299.1460318**; **Malcolm, Roseboom, Clark, Fazar, "Application of a Technique for Research and Development Program Evaluation," Operations Research 7(5):646–669, DOI 10.1287/opre.7.5.646** (PERT); **MacCrimmon & Ryavec, "An Analytical Study of the PERT Assumptions," Operations Research 12(1):16–37, DOI 10.1287/opre.12.1.16, 1964** (note: the FDPM's task description appears to conflate this 1964 paper with the 1959 PERT paper — correct the year); **Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, Stein, *Introduction to Algorithms*, 3e, MIT Press 2009, ISBN 978-0-262-03384-8**, §24.2 (DAG longest-path via topological sort + relaxation, Θ(V+E)).

The 1→2→…→22 longest serial chain is computable in Θ(V+E). **It is conditional on full read/write-set declaration per Bernstein 1966.** With write-sets alone, only WAW edges are generated; RAW and WAR — typically dominant in product development, where downstream phases read upstream artifacts without overwriting them — are invisible, so the computed DAG is a strict subgraph of the true dependency DAG and the claimed critical path is a *lower bound* on the actual one. **Action**: prominently flag the read-set caveat; publish the verified edge-list and run the Θ(V+E) algorithm rather than asserting the chain; provide a proof obligation for each parallel-track vertex v ∈ {6,10,11,15,17,18,19,20} that its longest path through 1→…→22 has length < 14; address probabilistic duration via PERT or Monte Carlo if any phase is uncertain (Jensen's inequality means deterministic CPM systematically under-estimates).

## 15. "Projection" is a category error — use "phase transition"

**Awodey, *Category Theory*, 2e, Oxford 2010, ISBN 978-0-19-923718-0**; **Halmos, *Introduction to Hilbert Space*, Chelsea 1951**; **Roman, *Advanced Linear Algebra*, 3e, Springer GTM 135, 2008, ISBN 978-0-387-72828-5**; lens references as in §4.

A projection in every relevant discipline (category theory, linear algebra, functional analysis) satisfies p∘p = p or is a product component map. The FDPM admits π_N is non-idempotent; calling it a projection invites the reader to apply theorems (spectral structure of projectors, splitting lemma, universal property of products) that simply do not hold. The FDPM's π_N is operationally a **state transformer** parameterized by Δ_N — closest to Haskell-lens `over l f s` than to `view l s`. **Action**: rename to **τ_N (phase transition)** or **σ_N (phase update operator)**; reserve π for true projections; if a lens analogy is desired, define a paired view_N and prove PutGet, otherwise drop it. State a transition-system semantics, not a projection semantics.

## 16. Single-writer ownership is necessary, not sufficient

**Conway, "How Do Committees Invent?" Datamation 14(4):28–31, 1968**; **Parnas, "On the Criteria to Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules," CACM 15(12):1053–1058, DOI 10.1145/361598.361623**; **Parnas, Clements, Weiss, "The Modular Structure of Complex Systems," IEEE TSE SE-11(3):259–266, DOI 10.1109/TSE.1985.232209**; **Lampson, "Hints for Computer System Design," SOSP 1983, DOI 10.1145/773379.806614**; **Brooks, *The Mythical Man-Month*, anniv. ed., Addison-Wesley 1995, ISBN 978-0-201-83595-3**; **Lieberherr & Holland, "Assuring Good Style for Object-Oriented Programs" (Law of Demeter), IEEE Software 6(5):38–48, DOI 10.1109/52.35588**; **Winters, Manshreck, Wright, *Software Engineering at Google*, O'Reilly 2020, ISBN 978-1-492-08279-8** (Hyrum's Law).

**Single-writer ownership is the W∩W = ∅ clause of Bernstein and nothing more.** The literature on hidden coupling makes plain why: ambient state — environment variables, feature flags, secrets, file system, system clock, RNG, network endpoints, logs, metrics, kernel page cache — escapes any static partition. None of it is owned by any phase, yet every phase reads it and many mutate it. Hyrum's Law closes the loop: even on-paper-disjoint partitions leak through observable side-effects (timing, log lines, error codes) that downstream phases come to depend on. **Action**: introduce an explicit ambient region S_amb that no phase exclusively owns and that every phase must declare reads/writes against (taxonomy: config, env, secrets, time, randomness, FS, network, telemetry, OS state); state Bernstein's conditions in full; adopt Parnas–Clements–Weiss module-guide discipline (secret hidden, uses-relation, ambient footprint); add a "no spooky action" invariant forbidding implicit back-channels.

## 17. 22 phases is engineering judgment — and the invariance is a feature

**McCabe, "A Complexity Measure," IEEE TSE SE-2(4):308–320, DOI 10.1109/TSE.1976.233837**; **Halstead, *Elements of Software Science*, Elsevier 1977, ISBN 0-444-00205-7**; **Curtis, Kellner, Over, "Process Modeling," CACM 35(9):75–90, DOI 10.1145/130994.130998**; **Fenton & Pfleeger, *Software Metrics*, 2e, PWS 1997, ISBN 0-534-95425-1**; **ISO/IEC 33020:2019**.

**Verdict: the weakest adversarial target.** No objective theory of "right" phase count exists; Curtis–Kellner–Over (1992) is explicit that granularity is engineering judgment tied to model purpose. The FDPM's concession that 17/19/26 yield the same critical path is, on rigorous reading, an unintentional **theorem statement**: the critical-path *length* is invariant under refinement that preserves the precedence DAG. **This is a positive structural property — own it, don't apologize for it.** Action: reframe the invariance as a theorem citing the partial-order semantics implicit in CPM; justify 22 specifically on Parnas-1972 grounds (each phase isolates a distinct change axis); distinguish latency invariance (length) from throughput invariance (width — splitting can expose intra-phase parallelism); use McCabe-style discipline as a heuristic upper bound on per-phase complexity; disclaim formal optimality.

## 18. Invariant monotonicity is AGM revision dressed as Lamport safety

**Lamport, "Proving the Correctness of Multiprocess Programs," IEEE TSE SE-3(2):125–143, DOI 10.1109/TSE.1977.229904**; **Alpern & Schneider, "Defining Liveness," IPL 21(4):181–185, DOI 10.1016/0020-0190(85)90056-0**; **Ernst et al., "The Daikon System for Dynamic Detection of Likely Invariants," Sci. Comp. Prog. 69(1–3):35–45, DOI 10.1016/j.scico.2007.01.015**; **Furia, Meyer, Velder, "Loop Invariants: Analysis, Classification, and Examples," ACM CSUR 46(3):34, DOI 10.1145/2506375**.

**Verdict: CHALLENGES.** "Once committed, ∀ M > N it holds in S_M unless an explicit amendment supersedes" is *not* a safety property in the technical sense — the escape clause makes it vacuous (any trace can satisfy it by amending immediately before any violation). The FDPM appears to claim temporal monotonicity (b) but only delivers latest-committed-value safety (a). **The actual semantics is AGM belief revision, not Lamport invariance.** Daikon and the loop-invariant literature show invariants in real systems are inferred and routinely revised — empirical reality the amendment mechanism responds to. **Action**: rename to "invariant persistence-under-explicit-revision" or "latest-committed-value safety"; cite AGM (next §) as the conceptual home; specify the enforcement mechanism (monotonic log + deterministic projection) for the safety reading to be model-checkable; distinguish governance (who may amend) from logic (the resulting non-monotonic revision).

## 19. Step 22 is not a fixed point — it is a reactive system

**Tarski, "A Lattice-Theoretical Fixpoint Theorem and its Applications," Pacific J. Math. 5(2):285–309, DOI 10.2140/pjm.1955.5.285**; **Cousot & Cousot, "Abstract Interpretation," POPL 1977, DOI 10.1145/512950.512973**; **Harel & Pnueli, "On the Development of Reactive Systems," NATO ASI F-13, Springer 1985, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-82453-1_17**; **Manna & Pnueli, *The Temporal Logic of Reactive and Concurrent Systems: Specification*, Springer 1992, ISBN 978-1-4612-8780-5, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4612-4222-2**; **Sangiorgi, "On the Origins of Bisimulation and Coinduction," ACM TOPLAS 31(4):15, DOI 10.1145/1516507.1516510**.

A fixed point of f is by definition x with f(x) = x. The FDPM recurrence S_{N>22} = π_22(S_{N-1}) ⊕ Δ_N has no such x — Δ_N is fresh per step, the right-hand side varies with N. **What the recurrence defines is an iteration / unbounded trace of a transition system — exactly Harel-Pnueli's *reactive system*, which "cannot be described adequately as computing a function from an initial state to a terminal state."** The misuse matters: "fixed point" carries termination, monotone convergence, and lattice-theoretic uniqueness commitments the FDPM has not earned. **Action**: strike "fixed-point operation"; reformulate as τ_22 : S × E → S with S_{N+1} = τ_22(S_N, Δ_{N+1}) — a non-terminating reactive operation in the Harel-Pnueli sense. State desired steady-state properties as temporal-logic invariants □I (Manna & Pnueli 1992), or as coinductive invariants νX. (I ∧ τ_22⁻¹(X)). If a true fixed-point semantics is genuinely wanted, declare a CPO on S, prove monotonicity, and cite Tarski/Cousot — substantial work.

## 20. The whole apparatus is bunched implications without naming it

Symbolic Logic 5(2):215–244, DOI 10.2307/421090**; **Pym, *The Semantics and Proof Theory of the Logic of Bunched Implications*, Kluwer Applied Logic Series 26, 2002, ISBN 1-4020-0745-0, DOI 10.1007/978-94-010-0091-7**; **Calcagno, O'Hearn, Yang, "Local Action and Abstract Separation Logic," LICS 2007, DOI 10.1109/LICS.2007.30**; **Hoare, Möller, Struth, Wehrman, "Concurrent Kleene Algebra," CONCUR 2009, LNCS 5710:399–414, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-04081-8_27**, with journal version *J. The FDPM's ⊕ is the separating conjunction; (Δ, ⊕, Δ_∅) is a separation algebra; the Bernstein condition is the disjointness predicate; the structural laws are the PCM laws verbatim. The "magic wand" P −∗ Q gives a frame-rule local-reasoning principle the FDPM is not exploiting. Concurrent Kleene Algebra (Hoare et al. 2009/2011) provides the two-operator structure — `;` for sequential `apply`, `‖` for parallel ⊕ — tied by an exchange law (a‖b);(c‖d) ≤ (a;c)‖(b;d) that is the algebraic counterpart of the FDPM's parallelism justification. **Action**: replace ad-hoc terminology with established names (PCM, separation algebra, BI, CKA); state the four PCM laws verbatim; adopt BI as the assertion logic; use CKA to handle the interaction of `apply` and ⊕; cite Brookes (2007) for soundness; acknowledge that full BI is undecidable so mechanizable claims must restrict to known-decidable fragments (symbolic heaps, bi-abduction, Calcagno et al., JACM 58(6), DOI 10.1145/2049697.2049700).

## 21. Limits of formalization — the rename is the right move

**Royce, "Managing the Development of Large Software Systems," IEEE WESCON 1970** (no DOI; the actual paper repeatedly calls single-pass waterfall "risky and invites failure"); **Parnas & Clements, "A Rational Design Process: How and Why to Fake It," IEEE TSE SE-12(2):251–257, DOI 10.1109/TSE.1986.6312940**; **Brooks, "No Silver Bullet," IEEE Computer 20(4):10–19, DOI 10.1109/MC.1987.1663532**; **Fuggetta, "Software Process: A Roadmap," ICSE FOSE 2000, DOI 10.1145/336512.336521**; **Glass, *Facts and Fallacies of Software Engineering*, Addison-Wesley 2003, ISBN 0-321-11742-5**; **Kruchten, *The Rational Unified Process*, 3e, Addison-Wesley 2003, ISBN 0-321-19770-4**; CMMI for Development v1.3, CMU/SEI-2010-TR-033; **Staples et al., "An exploratory study of why organizations do not adopt CMMI," JSS 80(6):883–895, DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2006.09.008**.

Parnas & Clements 1986 is the canonical formulation: real design is never the rational top-down derivation that documents purport to record; the proper response is to **fake it** — write documentation as though the process had been rational, while honestly acknowledging it was not. The FDPM's structure replicates this faked-rational form, which is *legitimate*. Brooks 1986 reinforces: process formalism attacks accidental, not essential, complexity. The empirical record (Fuggetta 2000, Staples et al. 2007) shows that process-centered environments fail to transfer to industry primarily because of documentation overhead. Boehm & Turner's "home ground" model (criticality, team size, personnel skill, requirements stability, culture) gives the criteria that justify heavyweight process. **The FDPM's rename from `formal-specification` to `structured-decomposition` is itself a textbook application of Parnas–Clements *inverted* — down-claiming to a label that accurately reflects what the artifact does, rather than overclaiming formality it does not possess.** Action: own the "fake it" framing rather than apologize; declare home ground in Boehm-Turner terms; cite Royce 1970 with the misreading hazard called out; **do not add more formalism in response to this review** — the literature's clearest finding is that marginal process formality past a certain point has negative value.

## 22. The supersession lifecycle is AGM revision and needs a transition table

**Alchourrón, Gärdenfors, Makinson, "On the Logic of Theory Change: Partial Meet Contraction and Revision Functions," J. Symbolic Logic 50(2):510–530, DOI 10.2307/2274239**; **Gärdenfors, *Knowledge in Flux*, MIT Press 1988, ISBN 0-262-07109-6**; **Hansson, *A Textbook of Belief Dynamics*, Kluwer Applied Logic 11, 1999, ISBN 978-0-7923-5329-4**; **Katsuno & Mendelzon, "On the Difference Between Updating a Knowledge Base and Revising It," KR'91:387–394**; **Harel, "Statecharts: A Visual Formalism for Complex Systems," Sci. Comp. Prog. 8(3):231–274, DOI 10.1016/0167-6423(87)90035-9**; **Holzmann, *The SPIN Model Checker*, Addison-Wesley 2003, ISBN 0-321-22862-6**.

The five-status lifecycle (verified | unverified | assumed | invalidated | superseded) is an **incompletely specified finite-state machine**: the alphabet of triggering events, the initial state, terminal markers, and the legality of edges (e.g., `verified → invalidated` under retracted evidence?) are not stated. The most plausible reconstruction is a Harel statechart with `unverified` initial; `unverified → {verified, assumed}` on evidence/acknowledgment; `{assumed, verified} → invalidated` on counter-evidence; `{any non-terminal} → superseded` on replacement with mandatory forward pointer; `{invalidated, superseded}` terminal. Mapping onto AGM is intellectually appealing but technically loose: AGM operates on belief sets, FDPM on items. The Katsuno–Mendelzon distinction between **update** (world changed; old assumption was correct then, wrong now → `superseded`) and **revision** (better information about an unchanged world → `invalidated`) is exactly the distinction the FDPM should make and currently does not. **Action**: publish a 5×5 transition table or Harel chart with permitted/forbidden cells and triggering events; distinguish `invalidated` (contraction) from `superseded` (revision/update); cite AGM as conceptual antecedent in a footnote with explicit disclaimer that postulates do not transfer; if any verification is desired, encode in TLA+ or Promela and check the obvious invariants (terminal states have no outgoing edges, every `superseded` has a non-null forward pointer, no cycles in the supersession graph). Keep `limitation:formalism-not-proof`.

## 23. UUIDv7 — supports, with a hot-spot caveat

**RFC 9562 (Davis, Peabody, Leach, May 2024), "Universally Unique IDentifiers (UUIDs)"**, obsoletes RFC 4122. **§5.7** specifies v7 layout (48-bit Unix-ms timestamp || version || sub-ms or counter || variant || random); **§6.2** Methods 1–3 for monotonic generation; **§6.13 "DBMS and Database Considerations"** — *not "Appendix B" as the task description states; RFC 9562 has no Appendix B*. **Winand, *SQL Performance Explained*, 2012, ISBN 978-3-9503078-2-5** and *Use the Index, Luke!* (use-the-index-luke.com). **PostgreSQL 18** (Sept 25, 2025) added native `uuidv7()` (commit 78c5e141e, Borodin); committer benchmark on M2 inserting 30M rows: uuidv4 ~2004 s, uuidv7 ~337 s (≈6× speedup). [Gray] Marc Brooker, "Fixing UUIDv7 for database use-cases," Oct 2025, brooker.co.za — flags **last-page contention** under high concurrency.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS, REFINES.** v7 delivers index locality and avoids B-tree fragmentation that v4 causes; the trade-off vs. sequential `bigint` is global uniqueness without coordination at modest extra storage. Action: cite §6.13 (correct the "Appendix B" reference); acknowledge last-page contention as a known concurrency trade-off; if Postgres-targeted, cite the PG-18 native function rather than recommending application-side generation; note that v7 leaks creation time (48-bit ms recoverable), unsuitable where this is sensitive.

## 24. Keyset pagination over (created_at, id) — supported

**Winand, *Use the Index, Luke!* — "Paging Through Results" / "no-offset campaign"** (use-the-index-luke.com/no-offset, /sql/partial-results/fetch-next-page); **Winand, *SQL Performance Explained*, 2012**; PostgreSQL row-wise comparison docs.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS.** Keyset is O(limit) given a matching index; OFFSET is O(skipped+limit) regardless of indexing — a known DoS vector for deep pagination. Refinements the FDPM should incorporate: cursors must encode the *full* tuple `(created_at, id)`, not just timestamp (Winand's deterministic-order requirement; Hubspot has documented bugs from infinite loops on duplicate timestamps); the composite index must match the ORDER BY column order and direction; `created_at` must be **immutable post-insert** or previously-issued cursors become ambiguous (this justifies I-07.03); use SQL row-value comparison `(created_at, id) < (?, ?)` rather than the broken `created_at < ? OR (created_at = ? AND id < ?)` form; document that random page jumps are a trade-off (Eder describes a cached-boundary hybrid).

## 25. Append-only audit logs — three enforcement levels, software alone is bypassable

**ISO/IEC 9075:2011 (SQL:2011)**, system-versioned tables — survey: **Kulkarni & Michels, "Temporal Features in SQL:2011," ACM SIGMOD Record 41(3), DOI 10.1145/2380776.2380786**; **Helland, "Immutability Changes Everything," CIDR 2015 / ACM Queue 13(9), DOI 10.1145/2814213.2884038**; **RFC 9162 (Laurie, Messeri, Stradling, Dec 2021), "Certificate Transparency Version 2.0"** — cryptographically enforced append-only via Merkle tree with inclusion and consistency proofs; **Vernon, *Implementing Domain-Driven Design*, Addison-Wesley 2013, ISBN 978-0-321-83457-7** (event sourcing). Allowing normal users to change their settings would defeat the point of an audit log."

**Verdict: REFINES, partially CHALLENGES.** Distinguish three levels: (a) software-only (triggers, system-versioned tables) — defeats casual mistakes only, bypassable by superuser; (b) WORM/append-only storage (S3 Object Lock, immutable filesystems) — defeats DBA tampering; (c) cryptographic Merkle log (RFC 9162) — defeats insider log-server tampering with external auditors. SQL:2011 is silent on tamper resistance; PGAudit's own docs concede the superuser issue. If the FDPM requires non-repudiable audit, hash chaining or Merkle logging is the canonical answer.

## 27. Refresh token rotation with reuse-canary — supported by RFC 9700

## 30. Single-writer in distributed systems — the biggest analogy hazard

**Thompson, Farley, Barker, Gee, Stewart, "Disruptor: High Performance Alternative to Bounded Queues," LMAX 2011** (lmax-exchange.github.io/disruptor); **Kreps, Narkhede, Rao, "Kafka: a Distributed Messaging System for Log Processing," NetDB 2011**; **Lamport, "On Interprocess Communication, Parts I & II," Distributed Computing 1(2):77–101, 1986, DOI 10.1007/BF01786227** (SWMR registers, regularity/atomicity hierarchy); **Helland, "Life Beyond Distributed Transactions," CIDR 2007 / ACM Queue 14(5):2016, DOI 10.1145/3009824** (entity ownership); Aeron (Real Logic, github.com/real-logic/aeron). [Gray] Thompson, "Single Writer Principle," Mechanical Sympathy blog 2011.

**Verdict: CHALLENGES — this is the most important correction in the empirical set.** The single-writer pattern in the runtime/distributed-systems literature is a **temporal, runtime-enforced** property: at any *instant*, exactly one thread/broker/process holds write authority; benefits accrue from cache-coherence physics or consensus mechanisms. The FDPM's "phase ownership" is a **static authoring discipline**: phase document P_k is the sole *source* permitted to specify state component S_k *during the development process*, enforced by code review/lint/convention. **The two are not the same property**, although both are colloquially called "single writer."

| Property | Disruptor / Kafka / Aeron | FDPM "phase ownership" |
|---|---|---|
| Time | runtime | authoring time |
| Enforcement | scheduler / broker / hardware | code review / lint / convention |
| Failure mode | data race, torn read | inconsistent specifications |
| Throughput concern | yes | no |

Action: be **explicit that the analogy is an analogy** ("structurally similar to, but operationally distinct from, the runtime single-writer principle"); the closest *philosophical* match is Helland 2007 entity ownership as a discipline, not Disruptor as a runtime pattern; cite Lamport 1986 only if making formal read/write consistency claims; do not claim Disruptor's "no contention / no synchronization" benefits — those are cache-coherence physics that do not transfer to a documentation discipline.

## What the formalism IS vs. what it CLAIMS TO BE

What the FDPM **actually is**, mathematically: a **partial commutative monoid** (Δ, ⊕, Δ_∅) — equivalently, a separation algebra in the sense of Calcagno–O'Hearn–Yang 2007 — over per-phase increments, with a partition-based ownership discipline that discharges the W∩W = ∅ clause of Bernstein 1966 over a finite-product state vector. Layered on top: a temporal workflow (in the SPEM / Little-JIL / Osterweil-process-formalism sense, cf. ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017) whose dependency DAG admits a Θ(V+E) longest-path computation conditional on full read/write-set declaration; an authoring-time single-writer discipline (a static analogue of Helland 2007 entity ownership, *not* a runtime analogue of Disruptor); and an assumption-supersession mechanism whose semantics are AGM belief revision (Alchourrón–Gärdenfors–Makinson 1985), not Lamport-style temporal monotonicity. Minimally rephrased in standard vocabulary, this is a **structured, traceable, partially-ordered authoring workflow with separation-style ownership and explicit revision semantics** — a real, non-trivial, useful artifact.

What the FDPM **claims to be but is not**: a "formal specification" with verification calculus (it has no semantic relation between specifications and no proof obligations); a *projection* apparatus (π_N is non-idempotent — a phase transition or lens-put, not a projection); a *fixed-point computation* (step 22 is a non-terminating reactive operation in the Harel–Pnueli sense, not a Tarski/Knaster fixed point); a *parallelism guarantee from single-writer ownership* (Bernstein requires read-sets, and ambient state — config, env, secrets, time, RNG — escapes any static partition); a *refinement chain* in the Wirth/Morgan/Back-von Wright/Event-B sense (these are vertical abstraction relations, not horizontal phase sequences; "step gates" are exit-criteria checklists, not predicate-transformer obligations); and a *monotonic-invariant discipline* in the Lamport / CALM / CRDT sense (the amendment clause is the non-monotone coordination point CALM identifies as requiring synchronization).

The cleanest editorial path forward is one paragraph in a Foundations section that names ⊕ a partial commutative monoid / separation algebra (Calcagno–O'Hearn–Yang 2007), names the assertion logic BI (O'Hearn–Pym 1999), names the parallel/sequential algebra CKA (Hoare et al. 2009), and names the operational model a workflow net (van der Aalst 1997). With those four citations the FDPM's informal claims become standard theorems with known decidability profiles, and the apparatus stops borrowing authority from formal-methods vocabulary it does not satisfy. The recent rename from `profile:formal-specification` to `profile:structured-decomposition` is exactly this honesty applied to the profile name — Parnas & Clements 1986 inverted, downclaiming to a label that fits — and should be defended in the document body, not buried in a limitations footnote. The remaining work is to apply the same honesty in five places: replace "projection" with "phase transition," replace "fixed point" with "reactive operation," strengthen "single-writer ownership" to "single-owner of read-write footprint" (with declared ambient region), rename "invariant_monotonicity" to "invariant persistence-under-explicit-revision," and replace "step gates as refinement obligations" with "stage gates as exit-criteria checklists." Each change is a one-line edit. Each closes a real, citable, semantic gap. None of them weakens the artifact; collectively they make it defensible against the literature it currently borrows from.
